## Policy

An equation is added only when it matches a published source. A set that cannot be checked is excluded and listed here.

For gli_2012, gli_2022 and nhanes3, the published papers are the original source. rspiro is the independent implementation used for the check.

## How to regenerate the JRS fixtures

Run this command from the repo root, so that .Rprofile activates renv:

```
Rscript streamlit/tests/r/make_jrs_fixtures.R
```

The script stops if it is not run from the repo root or if any value is NA. The script prints the rspiro version when it runs. Version used for the current fixture: rspiro 0.5. The script writes tests/testthat/fixtures/jrs_reference_cases.csv. All numbers come from rspiro. The file has 15 cases with 3 rows each (FEV1, FVC, FEV1FVC).

## JRS age facts

Checked on 2026-11-06 with rspiro 0.5 and pyspiro (class KUBOTA_2014). Both return the same values, and both return NA at the same ages.

- Men: a value for ages 17 to 95 (95 is valid, 95.01 is NA). NA at 16 and from 95.01.
- Women: a value for ages 17 to 93.99. NA from 94 (93.5 and 93.99 are valid, 94 and 95 are NA). NA at 16 and 96 for both sexes.
- The wrapper rejects an age where rspiro returns NA. For women the message says: For women, Kubota 2014 (JRS) covers ages below 94 years. The limit is exclusive (94 rejected, 93.99 accepted). The registry age_range stays (17, 95).
- Non-integer ages: rspiro floors the age only for the spline lookup. The LMS terms use the exact age. Age 30.7 gives a different value from age 30 (men, 170 cm, FEV1: 3.8351 L and 3.8623 L). The wrapper agrees with rspiro. test_kubota_non_integer_age_matches_rspiro pins this.
- rspiro takes height in metres. The wrapper takes centimetres.

## rspiro arguments

- `pred_JRS(age, height, gender = 1, param = "FEV1")`
- `LLN_JRS(age, height, gender = 1, param = "FEV1")`
- `zscore_JRS(age, height, gender = 1, FEV1 = NULL, FVC = NULL, VC = NULL, FEV1FVC = NULL)`
- `pctpred_JRS(age, height, gender = 1, FEV1 = NULL, FVC = NULL, VC = NULL, FEV1FVC = NULL)`
- Sex: `gender` is 1 for male and 2 for female.
- Height is in metres. Age is in years.
- FEV1/FVC is a fraction, not a percentage.
- `param` takes one of "FEV1", "FVC", "FEV1FVC".
- The fixture covers ages 17 to 95. rspiro returns NA for females older than 93, so the oldest female case is 90.

## NHANES III notes

Checked on 2026-11-06 against the paper: Hankinson JL, Odencrantz JR, Fedan KB. Spirometric reference values from a sample of the general U.S. population. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 1999;159(1):179-187, doi:10.1164/ajrccm.159.1.9712108, PMID 9872837. Read from the PDF on CDC Stacks, https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/198173/cdc_198173_DS1.pdf. The ATS journal page redirected to a publisher page and was not read. PubMed Central has no full text.

- Change point in the paper: journal page 184 says that the best change point was between 19 and 23 yr for males and between 17 and 21 yr for females, and that "20 yr was the best change point for males and 18 yr for females". Table 4 (male subjects) has the rows "< 20 yr of age" and ">= 20 yr of age" for each ethnicity. Table 5 (female subjects) has the rows "< 18 yr of age" and ">= 18 yr of age". Table 6 (FEV1/FVC% and FEV1/FEV6%) has no age groups.
- rspiro 0.5 (pred_NHANES3 and the other NHANES3 functions) uses the younger equations when age < 20, for both sexes.
- pyspiro 1.0.0 (HANKINSON_1999._age_group) uses the younger equations when age <= 19 for men and age <= 17 for women. For whole-year ages this is the rule of the paper. For a fractional age just below the change point it is not.
- The wrapper follows the paper. It uses a subclass of HANKINSON_1999 (_Hankinson1999PaperChangePoint in streamlit/quadrant/wrappers.py) that overrides the private method _age_group with the change points of the paper: younger equations below 20 years for men and below 18 years for women, for scalars and arrays. The coefficients are those of pyspiro; only the choice of age group changes. The change points are NHANES3_MALE_CHANGE_POINT_YEARS and NHANES3_FEMALE_CHANGE_POINT_YEARS in streamlit/quadrant/constants.py. Because _age_group is private, pyspiro stays pinned to >=1.0,<1.1, and test_pyspiro_private_age_group_is_still_what_the_wrapper_overrides fails if pyspiro changes its name, arguments or return values.
- Result by age (fixture nhanes3_age_split_cases.csv, ages 8, 17, 17.5, 18, 19, 19.5 and 20, both sexes, three ethnicities):
  - Men at every age, and women at 8, 17, 17.5 and 20 years: the wrapper, rspiro and the paper use the same equation. The wrapper matches rspiro within 0.01. This includes the fractional ages: a man of 19.5 years and a woman of 17.5 years get the younger equation. Without the override, pyspiro gives them the older equation, which differs from the paper by 0.04 to 0.08 L in FEV1 and 0.03 to 0.10 L in FVC.
  - Women 18: rspiro uses the younger equation, the wrapper and the paper the older one. The two equations meet at 18 years, so the values agree within 0.01.
  - Women 19 and 19.5: rspiro uses the younger equation, the wrapper and the paper the older one. FEV1 differs by 0.07 to 0.12 L and FVC by 0.05 to 0.14 L (woman 19 years, 165 cm, Caucasian: FEV1 wrapper 3.42 L, rspiro 3.50 L; FVC wrapper 3.89 L, rspiro 3.95 L). The R app uses rspiro, so it shows the rspiro value for women aged 18 to below 20 years.
  - FEV1/FVC: no age group, so the wrapper matches rspiro at every age.
- Tests: test_nhanes3_uses_the_equation_of_the_paper_around_the_change_point compares the wrapper with the independent module at the fixture ages, both sexes. test_nhanes3_age_split_matches_rspiro_except_where_rspiro_departs_from_the_paper compares it with rspiro and states the gap for women aged 18 to below 20 years.
- Possible follow-up (not done): the rule of the paper could be reported to pyspiro (fractional ages just below the change point) and to rspiro (women aged 18 to below 20 years).
- Independent check: streamlit/tests/independent/nhanes3_1999.py holds the Caucasian FEV1 and FVC rows of Tables 4 and 5, transcribed from the paper. With the age group that rspiro uses, it reproduces the rspiro values within 0.0001.
- To regenerate the fixture, run from the repo root: `Rscript streamlit/tests/r/make_nhanes3_age_split_fixtures.R` (rspiro 0.5).

## Pereira 2007 notes

Checked on 2026-11-06 against the English and Portuguese PDFs of the paper and the Table 4 image on the journal site. The three sources give the same values.

- Equations: linear in height (cm) and age (years), predicted = height coefficient x height + age coefficient x age + constant. The signs are part of the printed coefficients. Men are in Table 3, women in Table 4.
- Lower limit: the paper says that the lower limits "were calculated using the 5th percentile of the residual". The tables print it as P - x (men: FVC 0.90 L, FEV1 0.76 L, FEV1/FVC 7.6; women: FVC 0.64 L, FEV1 0.61 L, FEV1/FVC 8.50).
- No z-score: the lower limit is an empirical percentile, not predicted minus 1.645 times a residual standard deviation, and the paper gives no residual standard deviation. The wrapper does not derive a z-score. z_score is NaN and lln is set. pyspiro also returns NA for zscore() and lms() for this class.
- Classification (decision of the maintainer, 2026-11-06): the pattern comes from the observed values against the published lower limit. A value strictly below the lower limit counts as below the lower limit of normal, the same rule as z < -1.645 for the other equations; a value equal to the limit does not. Equal means within LIMIT_COMPARE_TOLERANCE (1e-9, streamlit/quadrant/constants.py): floating point puts the computed limit just above the printed one in 5669 of 16611 cases (integer ages and heights over both sampled ranges, three parameters), for example 3.2800000000000002 for 3.28 L (man, 27 years, 163 cm, FEV1), and the tolerance makes all of them equal. The z-score rule uses the same helper. FEV1/FVC is compared as a fraction on both sides (the wrapper divides the percent lower limit by 100). Severity is not graded, because ATS/ERS 2022 grades severity by the FEV1 z-score; the page and the report state "Severity not graded (no z-score for this equation)". interpret_spirometry returns basis "lower limit" for this case. The chart still leaves Pereira 2007 out, because it plots z-scores only. In the matrix, the LLN cell ends with an asterisk when the observed value is strictly below the lower limit, with the same rule as the classifier. Before this decision the pattern was not classified.
- Classification check: streamlit/tests/test_interpretation.py compares the pattern for five cases with a pattern worked by hand from the independent module (man 40 years, 170 cm, lower limits FEV1 3.189 L, FVC 3.881 L, FEV1/FVC 75.07 %: FEV1 2.7 L and FVC 4.0 L give obstructive, 3.5 and 4.5 L give normal; woman 50 years, 160 cm, lower limits FEV1 2.046 L, FVC 2.623 L, FEV1/FVC 72.70 %: 1.9 and 2.4 L give restrictive pattern (suggestive), 1.5 and 2.4 L give mixed pattern (suggestive), 2.0 and 2.65 L give non-specific). It also checks the age and height edges for each sex and an observed FVC exactly equal to its lower limit.
- Ratio units: the paper gives FEV1/FVC in percent. The wrapper returns the ratio, its predicted value and its lower limit as fractions.
- Weight: Table 3 also gives models with weight for men. The app collects no weight, so the wrapper uses the height and age models only (pyspiro parameters 1, 3 and 7, not the _WT parameters).
- Age and height: the registry range is 20 to 86 years. The sampled ranges differ by sex: men 26 to 86 years and 152 to 192 cm, women 20 to 85 years and 137 to 182 cm. The wrapper rejects inputs outside the range for the sex, with a message that names the sex and the limit. The limits are inclusive on the exact value. The rejection of a woman aged 85.5 is the pyspiro limit, which the wrapper follows. It is not a limit from the paper: the paper gives age at last birthday, so a woman aged 85.5 was 85 in the paper's terms.
- Predicted value: pyspiro lms() returns NA for this class, so the wrapper reads the predicted value from the pyspiro regression result (_scalar_regression, pyspiro 1.0.0). It does not derive it from percent(). Because this method is private, streamlit/pyproject.toml pins pyspiro to >=1.0,<1.1.
- pyspiro check: a one-off check on 2026-11-06 with pyspiro 1.0.0, not a test in the suite. Over 180 evaluations (both sexes, the age and height edges, three parameters) pyspiro and the independent module differ by less than 1e-13. The suite compares the wrapper with the independent module in 16 cases, including the height edges of the paper (men 152 and 192 cm, women 137 and 182 cm).

## ECCS 1993 notes

Checked on 2026-11-06 against the original source, tier 1 of Ruling 10: the ERS statement itself, read on the ERS publications site in two forms (the HTML full text and the scanned PDF of the printed supplement). The automated download of erj.ersjournals.com and publications.ersnet.org returned HTTP 403, so the pages were read in a browser. The HTML table and the scanned table give the same values. No published reproduction was needed for the status. pyspiro data and docstrings were not used as a source.

- Source pages: section 5.1 "Predictions for adults of Caucasian descent" (journal page 25, PDF page 21) Table 6, summary equations for lung volumes and ventilatory flows for adults aged 18 to 70 yr (journal page 26, PDF page 22), and section 6.3 "Reference values" (journal page 29).
- Equations: predicted = height coefficient x H + age coefficient x A + constant, with H the standing height in metres and A the age in years. The signs are part of the printed coefficients.
  - Men: FVC = 5.76H - 0.026A - 4.34 (l). FEV1 = 4.30H - 0.029A - 2.49 (l). FEV1/VC = -0.18A + 87.21 (%).
  - The unit of the men's FEV1 is printed as l/s (l·s-1). This is a misprint: FEV1 is a volume, and the women's FEV1 row prints l.
  - Women: FVC = 4.43H - 0.026A - 2.89 (l). FEV1 = 3.95H - 0.025A - 2.60 (l). FEV1/FVC = -0.19A + 89.10 (%).
- What is given and what is not: Table 6 gives the predicted mean, the residual standard deviation (RSD) and 1.64 RSD for each equation. This version uses the predicted mean only. lln and z_score are NaN, and the pattern is not classified: the page and the report show "Not classified (no lower limit and no z-score for this equation)".
- Age floor: the title of Table 6 has a footnote, "Between 18 and 25 yr substitute 25 yr in the equations". Section 5.1 says that between 18 and 25 yr an age of 25 yr is to be entered into the various equations. Section 6.3 repeats the rule: in subjects aged 18 to 25 yr the predicted mean is the same as for a subject aged 25 yr, so 25 yr is substituted in the prediction equations. The source names no exception by sex or by parameter. The floor applies to FVC, FEV1 and the ratio, for men and for women.
- pyspiro difference: pyspiro 1.0.0 (ECCS_1993) applies the floor to all parameters for women, but for men only to FEF75, FEF25_75, PEFR and FIVC. For men aged 18 to 25 its FVC, FEV1 and ratio use the real age. Example, man, 18 years, 170 cm: pyspiro FVC 4.984 l, source 4.802 l; FEV1 4.298 l, source 4.095 l; ratio 83.97 %, source 82.71 %. The wrapper passes max(age, 25) to pyspiro, so the result follows the source for both sexes. test_eccs_age_floor_below_25 pins this. The difference can be reported upstream.
- pyspiro check: a one-off check on 2026-11-06 with pyspiro 1.0.0, not a test in the suite. pyspiro holds the height coefficients per centimetre (the source values divided by 100). With the floored age, pyspiro and the independent module differ by less than 1e-14 over 288 evaluations (both sexes, eight ages from 18 to 70, six heights per sex including the range edges, three parameters).
- Ratio units: the source gives the ratio in percent. The wrapper returns the ratio and its predicted value as fractions.
- Ratio label: Table 6 labels the men's ratio "FEV1/VC" and the women's ratio "FEV1/FVC". The text of the statement uses FEV1/VC and recommends the inspiratory VC as the denominator. The wrapper uses both rows as the FEV1/FVC equation, as pyspiro does. For men the published equation is for FEV1/VC, so the predicted FEV1/FVC for men is an approximation of what the source defines. The maintainer can decide whether to keep the ratio row for men. Decision of the maintainer, 2026-11-06: the ratio row for men stays, and the registry caveat states that the source gives the men's ratio as FEV1/VC.
- Age and height: 18 to 70 years (Table 6 title and section 5.1). Section 5.1 gives the height range of the source data as 1.55 to 1.95 m in men and 1.45 to 1.80 m in women (the HTML full text says females, the scanned print says women). Section 6.3 gives the same ranges. The wrapper rejects inputs outside these ranges with a message that names the equation (and the sex for height). The limits are inclusive. pyspiro uses the same ranges.
- Population: the source says the equations apply to men and women of European descent; the section heading says "Caucasian descent". The registry says "Adults of European descent".
- Predicted value: pyspiro lms() returns NA for this class, so the wrapper reads the predicted value from the pyspiro regression result (_scalar_regression, pyspiro 1.0.0, pinned to >=1.0,<1.1), as for Pereira 2007. It does not derive it from percent().
- Corrections: Europe PMC lists one comment on the statement (Eur Respir J 1994;7(6):1197-8) and no erratum.

Possible follow-up (not done in this task): Table 6 states that "The lower 5 or upper 95 percentiles are obtained by subtracting or adding the figure in the last column from the predicted mean" (the last column is 1.64 RSD). Section 6.3 gives the same rule: the lower 5 per cent limit is the predicted mean minus 1.64·RSD, and it calls this the preferred method of delineating a reference limit. RSD and 1.64 RSD as printed: men FVC 0.61 and 1.00 l, FEV1 0.51 and 0.84 l, FEV1/VC 7.17 and 11.8 %; women FVC 0.43 and 0.71 l, FEV1 0.38 and 0.62 l, FEV1/FVC 6.51 and 10.7 %. With these, a lower limit (predicted minus 1.64 RSD) and a z-score ((observed minus predicted) / RSD) could be added. That is a decision for the maintainer.

## Jian 2017 notes

Checked on 2026-11-06 against the paper on PubMed Central (HTML tables) and the table images on the journal site. The two sources give the same values. Status: excluded. pyspiro data and docstrings were not used as a source.

- Citation: Jian W, Gao Y, Hao C, et al. Reference values for spirometry in Chinese aged 4-80 years. J Thorac Dis 2017;9(11):4538-4549, doi:10.21037/jtd.2017.10.110, PMID 29268524, PMCID PMC5720964. The pyspiro 1.0.0 docstring gives PMID 29268393. That PMID is a different article; the DOI in the docstring is correct.
- What the paper prints: Table S3 gives the LMS equations (M, S and L, and LLN = exp[ln(M) + ln(1 - 1.645 x L x S)/L]). Tables S1 (men) and S2 (women) give Mspline, Sspline and Lspline at 0.2-year steps from 4.0 to 81.0 years, rounded to 3 decimals. FEV1/FVC is in percent. Table 3 and Table 4 give predicted values for "the present study" at stated ages and heights. The paper prints no lower limit as a number; Figure 5 shows the lower limits as curves, at the mean height for age.
- Lookup tables: the pyspiro tables and coefficients are identical to Tables S1 to S3 (11,580 cells, no difference).
- Predicted values: 36 published values (Table 3: ages 20, 40 and 60 years, men 170 cm, women 160 cm; Table 4: ages 6, 10 and 14 years, men 117, 140 and 164 cm, women 117, 141 and 158 cm). Print rounding: FVC and FEV1 to 0.01 L (tolerance 0.005 L), FEV1/FVC to 0.1 percentage point (tolerance 0.05). 27 values are inside this tolerance. 9 are outside: men 60 years FVC 3.98 (pyspiro 3.9863) and FEV1/FVC 79.5 (79.427); men 10 years FEV1 2.03 (2.0242); men 14 years FVC 3.78 (3.7861); women 20 years FEV1/FVC 90.4 (90.471); women 40 years FEV1/FVC 83.1 (83.169); women 6 years FEV1/FVC 92.5 (92.422); women 10 years FEV1/FVC 90.8 (90.886). One possible cause is the 3-decimal rounding of the printed Mspline (up to 0.05 % of the value). All 9 are inside the tolerance when that is added, but this cannot be shown, because the unrounded splines are not published. Under the project rule the mismatch stands.
- Lower limit, internal conflict: for FVC in men, Table S3 gives L = 2.1201 - 0.4893 x ln(age), with no Lspline, but Table S1 gives a non-zero Lspline for FVC in men (0.870 at 4 years, -0.400 at 20 years, 0.818 at 80 years). For PEF in men it is the other way round: Table S3 adds an Lspline and Table S1 gives 0.000 at all ages. pyspiro adds the Lspline of Table S1 in every case.
- Figure 5 check: for FVC in men, S and L have no height term, so LLN/M depends on age only and can be read from Figure 5 without the height. The calibration panels (FEV1 in men and in women, where the tables have no conflict) agree with the tables within 0.003 in LLN/M for each decade. For FVC in men, Figure 5 gives LLN/M 0.838 at 20 to 29 years and 0.798 at 70 to 79 years; the tables give 0.831 and 0.808 with the Lspline, 0.827 and 0.821 without it. Neither reading reproduces the figure. For FEV1/FVC in women (also height-independent), the figure is closer to the reading without the Lspline (root mean square difference 0.0017) than to the printed equation with it (0.0030); this difference is small and is not used for the decision.
- Decision: the lower limit and the z-score of FVC in men cannot be traced to the paper, and the paper itself is not consistent. Also, 9 of the 36 predicted values in Tables 3 and 4 are outside the print rounding. The set is excluded. Not added: wrapper, registry row, tests, constants.

## Jo 2018 notes

Checked on 2026-11-06 against the paper on PubMed Central, its supplementary files and the figure images on the journal site. Status: excluded. pyspiro data and docstrings were not used as a source.

- Citation: Jo BS, Myong JP, Rhee CK, Yoon HK, Koo JW, Kim HR. Reference values for spirometry derived using Lambda, Mu, Sigma (LMS) method in Korean adults: in comparison with previous references. J Korean Med Sci 2018;33(3):e16, doi:10.3346/jkms.2018.33.e16, PMID 29215803, PMCID PMC5729644. Volume 33, issue 3, article e16 (the task brief gave issue 4, e11, which is wrong). Europe PMC lists no correction.
- What the paper prints: Table 2 gives M, S and L for FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC by sex (height in cm, age in years). Supplementary Tables 2 to 4 (xls files) give Mspline and Sspline for each whole year from 19 to 90 years, and an Lspline for FEV1/FVC in women (about -9e-14, in effect zero). FEV1/FVC is a fraction. The Methods give LLN = e{ln(M) + ln(1 - 1.645 x L x S)/L}, the 5th percentile. The paper prints no predicted value or lower limit as a number. Figure 2, Figure 3 and the graphical abstract show them as curves for men of 170 cm and women of 160 cm.
- Tables: the pyspiro coefficients are identical to Table 2 (text of the article and the table image), and the pyspiro lookup tables are identical to Supplementary Tables 2 to 4 (936 cells, no difference).
- Figure 2 check (digitized from the 1022 x 979 image, ages 20 to 90 in 5-year steps): FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC in men, and FEV1 and FVC in women, agree with pyspiro for the predicted value and the lower limit, within about 0.03 L for volumes and 0.008 for the ratio. Two points where the dashed 2013MRA line lies on the curve are not counted.
- Conflict: for FEV1/FVC in women, the lower limit from the printed Table 2 does not match the paper. At 160 cm, Figure 2 shows a lower limit of 0.711 at 20 years, 0.744 at 40 years, 0.699 at 60 years and 0.611 at 85 years. Table 2, as printed and as in pyspiro, gives 0.799, 0.792, 0.752 and 0.688. Figure 3, digitized in the same way, gives the same curve as Figure 2 (within 0.012), and the graphical abstract shows the same curve by eye. The Discussion says that the lower limit of FEV1/FVC in women falls below 0.70 at about 60 years; Table 2 gives 80.4 years. The predicted value (M) of the ratio in women matches the figure within 0.002, so the difference is in S or L.
- Probable cause: Table 2 prints the S equation of FEV1/FVC in women as exp{-3.11659 - 0.09128 x ln(A) + s-s}. With +0.09128 the result matches Figure 2 within 0.012 and Figure 3 within 0.008 at every age checked (0.710 at 20 years, 0.742 at 40 years, 0.700 at 60 years, 0.610 at 85 years) and falls below 0.70 at 60.0 years, as the text says. This points to a sign error in the printed coefficient. This project does not change a published coefficient, and pyspiro holds the printed value. A correction needs the authors' confirmation or an erratum.
- Decision: the set is excluded, because the lower limit and z-score of FEV1/FVC in women would contradict the paper's own figures and text. Not added: wrapper, registry row, tests, constants. The finding can be reported to the authors and upstream to pyspiro.
